JUNE 2026 · SWARM COLLECTION · SUNBURY

A new colony begins with a swarm.

In June, we were contacted about a large swarm of honey bees that had gathered in a tree in Sunbury. Thousands of bees had clustered together around their queen, forming the characteristic mass that can appear when a colony swarms.

Swarming bees are often relatively calm, but this particular colony was more defensive than usual, so protective clothing was used while collecting them. A smoker was also used to help keep the bees calm while they were carefully transferred.

The aim was to get the queen safely into the small transport hive. Once the queen was inside, the rest of the colony began following her in through the entrance. With so many bees trying to enter at once, they had to be gently guided and allowed time to make their way inside.

Once the colony had settled into the transport hive, it could be closed securely and moved to its new permanent home — turning a swarm found in a tree in Sunbury into another Zenny’s Bees colony.
